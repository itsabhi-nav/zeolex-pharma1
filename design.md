# 5. MOTION DESIGN: GIVE THE WEBSITE A RHYTHM

Motion must be a designed system, not a collection of random animations.

Develop a consistent motion language for the entire website.

## Required motion categories

### Entrance motion

Use carefully timed reveals for:

- Major headlines.
- Supporting copy.
- Product imagery.
- Technical diagrams.
- Section markers.
- Important calls to action.

Explore clipping masks, restrained vertical movement, opacity changes, and staggered typography.

### Scroll-driven storytelling

Create selected sections in which the content changes as the visitor scrolls.

Potential examples:

- The four delivery formats introduced sequentially.
- A visual progression from raw ingredients to finished product.
- A six-step manufacturing partnership journey.
- Layered scientific diagrams that reveal their components.
- A product development sequence from concept to packaged product.

Use sticky or pinned sections selectively.

Never make the entire website feel trapped inside a scrolling animation.

### Hover and interaction motion

Design polished interactions for:

- Navigation links.
- Buttons.
- Technology selectors.
- Product category rows.
- Image frames.
- Cards where cards are genuinely appropriate.
- Expandable FAQs.
- Form controls.
- Interactive diagrams.

Use subtle changes in position, scale, colour, clipping, or visual focus.

Avoid excessive cursor-following effects, fake magnetic movement, and animations that interfere with clicking or reading.

### Page transitions

If implementing route transitions, make them fast, elegant, and robust.

Do not compromise normal navigation, accessibility, browser history, or direct URL access.

### Motion principles

Every animation must serve at least one of these purposes:

- Establish hierarchy.
- Reveal information.
- Communicate a process.
- Explain a product or technology.
- Improve interaction feedback.
- Create a meaningful transition.

Use consistent easing, duration, and stagger tokens.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Avoid scroll hijacking, forced delays, excessive parallax, and animations that make the site feel sluggish.
